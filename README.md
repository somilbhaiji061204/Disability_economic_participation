# Economic Participation of Persons with Disabilities in India

## Overview

This project examines the economic participation of persons with disabilities in India, with particular attention to differences by age, sex, rural–urban residence, disability type, and State/Union Territory.

The analysis uses Census of India data from Table C-23, *Disabled Population among Main Workers, Marginal Workers, Non-Workers by Type of Disability, Age and Sex*.

The project was developed in R with the aim of producing a reproducible workflow for cleaning, analysing, and visualising Census data.

## Research Question

How do age, sex, disability type, and rural–urban residence shape the economic participation of persons with disabilities in India?

## Objectives

The analysis aims to:

1. Describe the demographic profile of the disabled population by sex, age group, and disability type.
2. Measure economic participation among persons with disabilities.
3. Compare economic participation across age groups, sex, rural–urban residence, and disability types.
4. Examine variation in economic participation across States and Union Territories.
5. Assess the association between demographic/geographic characteristics and worker status using chi-square tests and Cramér's V.

## Data

### Source

Census of India, Table C-23:

**Disabled Population among Main Workers, Marginal Workers, Non-Workers by Type of Disability, Age and Sex**

The original Census data file used for the analysis is stored locally in:

`data/raw/`

### Unit of analysis

The dataset consists of aggregated Census counts rather than individual-level observations. Therefore, the analysis treats the Census categories as population counts and does not treat individual rows as independent survey respondents.

### Key dimensions

The dataset contains information on:

- State/Union Territory
- District
- Rural/Urban residence
- Disability type
- Age group
- Sex
- Main workers
- Marginal workers
- Non-workers

## Data Cleaning

The original Census table contains a multi-row header structure. The R workflow:

1. Removes the original header rows from the imported worksheet.
2. Assigns meaningful variable names.
3. Converts population and worker-count variables to numeric format.
4. Checks for missing values and duplicated observations.
5. Validates the internal consistency of sex-specific and worker-specific counts.
6. Constructs derived measures required for the analysis.

The original Census file is retained separately from the cleaned analysis workflow.

## Economic Participation Measure

Economic participation is calculated as:

**Economic Participation Rate = (Main Workers + Marginal Workers) / Disabled Population × 100**

Marginal workers are calculated by combining:

- Marginal workers working for less than 3 months
- Marginal workers working for 3–6 months

Non-worker rates are also calculated for comparison.

## Statistical Analysis

The project uses descriptive and association-based statistical methods appropriate for aggregated Census data.

### Descriptive analysis

Economic participation rates are compared across:

- Age groups
- Sex
- Rural–urban residence
- Disability types
- States and Union Territories

### Chi-square tests

Chi-square tests are used to examine associations between worker status and:

- Sex
- Age group
- Rural–urban residence
- Disability type

### Effect size

Cramér's V is reported alongside the chi-square statistics to describe the strength of association.

Because the Census represents a very large population rather than a sample, substantive interpretation emphasizes effect sizes and percentage-point differences rather than statistical significance alone.

## Main Findings

The analysis indicates substantial differences in economic participation across demographic and disability characteristics.

- Economic participation is highest among persons aged **15–59 years**, followed by those aged **60+**.
- Male persons with disabilities have substantially higher economic participation than female persons with disabilities.
- Rural economic participation is somewhat higher than urban participation at the national level.
- Economic participation varies considerably across disability types.
- Mental-retardation, mental-illness, and multiple-disability groups show lower participation rates than several other disability categories.
- Economic participation also varies substantially across States and Union Territories.

Detailed results are provided in the tables and figures generated by the R workflow.
